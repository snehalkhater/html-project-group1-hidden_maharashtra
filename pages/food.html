Explore the Traditional Food of Maharashtra

Discover the delicious flavours of Maharashtra, from spicy street food and traditional meals to sweet delicacies. Explore the state's rich culinary heritage and enjoy the unique tastes of different regions.

Zunka Bhakri

zunka is an authentic Maharashtrian delicacy which is slightly spicy but very satisfying in texture, flavour and aroma. Learn how to make Marathi zunka bhakar.Zunka Bhakri is one of the most popular traditional dishes of Maharashtra, especially in rural areas. Zunka is a dry and spicy dish prepared using gram flour (besan), onions, green chillies, garlic, mustard seeds, and other spices. Bhakri is a round flatbread commonly made from jowar or bajra flour. The zunka is cooked until it becomes thick and crumbly, giving it a distinctive texture and flavour. This dish is often served with green chilli thecha, raw onions, and sometimes a little pickle. Zunka Bhakri is an important part of Maharashtra's rural food culture because it uses simple, locally available ingredients to create a satisfying meal. It is enjoyed for lunch or dinner and represents the traditional cooking practices of Maharashtra.

Pithla Bhakri

Pithla Bhakri is a traditional Maharashtrian meal loved for its simple ingredients and delicious taste. Pithla is prepared using gram flour, water, onions, green chillies, garlic, turmeric, and spices. It is cooked until it develops a soft, thick, curry-like consistency. Bhakri, made from jowar or bajra flour, is served alongside the pithla. The combination is commonly enjoyed with thecha, fresh onions, and green chillies. Pithla Bhakri is particularly popular in rural Maharashtra, where grains and gram flour are widely used in everyday cooking. It is an example of how ordinary ingredients can be transformed into a flavourful traditional meal. Although pithla and zunka use similar ingredients, pithla is generally softer and moister, while zunka is usually drier.

Puran Poli

Puran Poli is a traditional Maharashtrian sweet flatbread prepared with a filling of cooked chana dal, jaggery, and aromatic spices.It is the special dish of Maharashtra which is prepared on every occasion at every house, especially during festivals such as Gudhi Padwa, Akshaya Tritiya, Ganesh Chaturthi and Holi. It is eaten with Basundi, Aamras, Kadhi, Amti, etc. In Maharashtra, Puran Poli is eaten with a variant of Amti (flavored sour curry) known as Katachi Amti which is prepared with the remaining water of cooked Chana Dal used to make Puran Poli

It is commonly prepared during festivals such as Holi and Gudi Padwa. Puran Poli is often served with ghee and is loved for its sweet flavour.Maharashtrian puran poli is eaten on festive occasions like Gudi Padwa and Holi where it is cooked by almost every Maharashtrian house. No festive menu in Maharashtra is complete without Maharashtrian puran poli!

Thalipeeth

Thalipeeth, a traditional multi-grain flatbread, is a culinary gem of Mumbai, Maharashtra, celebrated for its rich flavors and nutritional value. This dish not only represents the vibrant culture of the city but also showcases the region's unique agricultural heritage.Thalipeeth or thalipith is a savoury multi-grain flatbread popular in Western India, particularly in the state of Maharashtra. The flour for thalipeeth, called bhajanee, is prepared from roasted grains, legumes and spices. The ingredients include grains such as rice, wheat, bajra, and jowar; legumes such as chana, and urad; and spices, most commonly coriander and cumin seeds.

Thalipeeth is more than just a dish; it embodies the spirit of Mumbai, where bustling breakfast stalls offer this wholesome delicacy to countless commuters. Traditionally prepared using a variety of flours and spices, it serves as a symbol of local identity, reflecting the diverse communities that call Mumbai home.

Bharli Vangi

Bharli Vangi is a well-known traditional Maharashtrian dish made from small brinjals, also called eggplants, stuffed with a flavourful spice mixture. The stuffing commonly includes roasted peanuts, grated coconut, sesame seeds, goda masala, chilli powder, and other spices, although recipes vary between families and regions. The stuffed brinjals are cooked slowly in a pan with oil and seasoning until they become tender and absorb the rich flavours of the masala. Some versions include tamarind or jaggery to create a balance of tangy, spicy, and slightly sweet flavours. Bharli Vangi is commonly served with jowar bhakri, bajra bhakri, chapati, or rice. It is a popular part of traditional Maharashtrian meals and showcases the region's use of local vegetables, peanuts, coconut, and aromatic spices.
